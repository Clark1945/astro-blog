- 2026 will be the year AI agents explode. Software engineers are finished.
- Don't bother learning X. AI is so powerful now — just have it do the work.

The way I see it, the stretch from 2025 to 2026 has been one long storm. The information online is wildly contradictory: some say AI can't replace software engineers, some say engineers don't write code anymore, some say software engineers are on the front line of being replaced, some say AI only produces something that _looks_ right while being riddled with holes underneath.

I'm not here today to settle the matter. I don't want to — and I'm not interested in — going through each claim to check who's right and who's wrong. The AI era has already given us far too much data for that.

I have exactly one point: have you actually touched AI today?

Some people work like this. They watched AlphaGo rise and figured software engineers were about to be replaced. They watched ChatGPT appear and figured software engineers were about to be replaced. They watched Claude Code take off and figured software engineers were about to be replaced. In this exploding era, all it takes is a rustle in the grass, and they mistake their own wavering for foresight.

Real business works like this: as long as there's one piece that doesn't work, that piece can't be replaced yet. And whether that piece is hard or easy — nobody knows.

But people writing for clicks work like this: they _have_ to posit a future of unlimited possibility, otherwise there's no story. Commentary that sticks to the facts — correctness aside — simply gets no traffic. As for whether their imagination is credible enough, nobody verifies it and nobody blames them afterward when they're wrong. The ethics of spreading information sit entirely in individual hands, and that has always been hard to enforce.

That said, some of the people raising the possibility of being replaced genuinely work in this industry. They're not wrong either — it's just that their words get read in ways that aren't accurate.

A Silicon Valley engineer earning NT$10 million a year facing layoff risk, and you, the MIS software engineer on NT$35,000 a month — are those really the same thing? Does the vantage point and the anxiety of top-tier talent actually intersect with your situation at all? Aren't you thinking a little too highly of yourself?

Instead of consuming piles of this will-AI-replace-us information garbage — and I'm not saying the information is wrong, I'm saying it's useless _to you_ — what you should be doing is gradually catching up with the actual thing. What AI can do, what it can't. Your work isn't the same as someone else's, your AI model isn't the same as someone else's — so what makes you so sure you'd both arrive at the same answer?

The road you've walked yourself is the only one that's real. Stop watching all that noise. Focus on learning and understanding, get a grip on this stuff step by step, and you'll end up with something genuinely valuable: an answer that's actually yours.

And one last time: don't mistake the silhouette under the spotlight for your own story. Live well.

That's all. Thank you.
