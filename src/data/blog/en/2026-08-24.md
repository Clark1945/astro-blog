## Foreword

"Spiritual fulfillment" — I'm sure you've heard the phrase plenty of times these past few years. It comes in many variants: work-life balance, "what you really want," and so on. But every time someone says it, it sounds lofty and leaves you completely in the dark.
You listen to a whole speech and come away with nothing. In the end, in this modern flood of information, we tend to flush that confusion down the toilet like a piece of tissue paper — but it matters. It matters a lot more than tissue paper.

Honestly, that confusion is perfectly normal. Someone who doesn't usually think along these lines will get a mild headache and then go back to their video games. But we can't do that. We have to go one step further.

To be fair, when courses and articles use vague wording, they aren't trying to mess with you. The term's definition genuinely is fuzzy and varies from person to person. Rather than handing you a clear-cut answer that might well be wrong, giving you a thinking framework so you can derive your own answer is the more appropriate approach.

Oh, and no, I'm not selling a course.

Anyway, I pulled together material from various sources and organized it into a rough framework with five components:

- A sense of giving
- Continuous growth
- Self-awareness
- Deep connection
- A sense of meaning

There's a good chance you'll still be lost after reading that list. I understand — I really do. But the problem probably isn't me; it's whether you've genuinely thought this through. Let me take them one at a time.

---

## A Sense of Giving

### What it is

A sense of giving refers to the feeling that "I'm capable of putting something out into the world, and what I put out was accepted." Contributing to others — that might be disadvantaged groups, your mistress (better not to have one), your wife, your kids, or your students if you happen to be a teacher.

### Why it matters

I'll be honest: you're probably more fragile than you think. Living in society, we're all chasing other people's recognition to some degree, and that connection shapes our social standing and self-perception. Once we lose it, we feel uncomfortable, even afraid.
That's why so many people become low after retiring — income isn't the issue, but the emotional value is gone, and they no longer feel "needed."

### How to do it

Help others. Any form works. For example, exactly what I'm doing right now — writing an article that takes the concept of spiritual fulfillment apart and lays it out for you.

## Continuous Growth

### What it is

Continuous growth is the conviction that "what I can do today is a little more than what I could do yesterday." The key point is that **you have to feel yourself growing**. If you're just busy with a pile of miscellaneous tasks and don't even believe you're improving, it probably isn't helping.

### Why it matters

We're afraid of stagnation. The overwhelming information anxiety makes us fear that AI will replace humans one day, or that our boss has hired someone to take over our job. People's tolerance for "standing still" is far lower than you'd expect. We want to not stay the same, which makes growing on multiple fronts genuinely important.

Growth brings more than capability — more importantly, it brings **possibility**. When you know you're still getting stronger, the future is open; when you feel you've stopped growing, the future turns into a track that's already been laid down for you.

### How to do it

Here are some criteria you can use:

- **Measurable**: Turn growth into something you can see. Don't rely on feelings or memory — keep records.
- **Difficult**: Staying in your comfort zone drilling the same thing doesn't count as growth. Go after challenges.
- **Output-producing**: Learning that's input-only easily creates illusions. You finish a book and think you understood it; you write notes on it and discover you didn't understand it at all. That's exactly why I organize my reading notes into posts.

By the way, growth doesn't have to be technical skill. Learning a new language, picking up a sport, getting good at cooking — they all count. What matters is that the trail of "here's one more thing I can do now" hasn't been broken.

## Self-Awareness

### What it is

Self-awareness means understanding yourself — including your emotions toward various things, the motives behind your behavior, and your values. It gives you the feeling that you aren't clueless, that you aren't lost at life's crossroads, and from that, confidence follows.

### Why it matters

A person without awareness gets pushed through life by reflexes. Your manager says something that annoys you, you fire back something even nastier, and then you spend three days handling the fallout.
You think you're angry, that your boss doesn't understand you — but you may never have examined your own emotions, or what that knot actually comes down to. And when you don't understand the problem, it's very hard to avoid repeating it.

### How to do it

I'll be upfront: this varies from person to person. I can give you a few AI-recommended methods, but I'm certain they won't be a 100% fit for you:

- **Write it down**: In your head, an emotion is a cloud of fog; put it into words and it becomes an object. It doesn't have to be a formal journal — one line like "today XX really got to me" is enough.
- **Ask the second layer of "why"**: "I'm annoyed" → "why am I annoyed" → "because my code review got shot down" → "why does that bother me so much" → "because I feel he's questioning my ability." Usually you only hit the real thing around the third layer.
- **Review regularly**: Look back at your records weekly or monthly, and you'll find the things that irritate you come down to just a handful of recurring patterns.

## Deep Connection

### What it is

With people, it means forming close friendships, a spouse, children. With things, it might be mountain climbing, keeping a pet, or faith. It means binding your emotions deeply to something, so they don't drift with the wind day after day.

### Why it matters

Because that kind of relationship is more resilient, and in the moments that matter it becomes the harbor for the unease in your heart.

### How to do it

I can't and don't know how to teach you to make friends, so here are some AI examples to suffer through on your own:

- **Lower your own defenses first**: Deep connection doesn't happen out of nowhere; someone always has to show vulnerability first. If you only ever share good news, the other person will only ever share good news back.
- **Invest in a small number of people**: Concentrating your time on three to five people beats spreading it evenly across fifty.
- **Maintain frequency, not intensity**: A deep conversation every two months works better than one big gathering a year.

## A Sense of Meaning

### What it is

This is the hardest one, and the most personal, because it's deeply tied to individual values — which makes it very hard to explain in a few sentences.

A sense of meaning is the answer to "what am I doing all of this for?" You can think about it along three lines:

- Is there a goal you're working toward?
- Do you believe the goal you're working toward is important?
- Are you consistent about it, rather than wavering back and forth?

### Why it matters

Growth without meaning turns into anxiety. Giving without meaning turns into depletion. Effort without meaning collapses the instant the goal is achieved — which is exactly why so many people, after landing the position they wanted or buying the house they wanted, fall into an emptiness they can't quite name. They've been substituting goals for meaning all along, and once the goal is met, there's nothing left holding it up.

### How to do it

Meaning can't be found; it can only be grown. But here are a few directions worth trying:

- **Start from who you affect**: Whose life is better because of what you do? If the answer is "nobody," you may genuinely need to do something else.
- **Start from coherence**: Line up the things you've done in the past and see whether there's a thread running through them. A person's sense of meaning often only surfaces in hindsight.
- **Accept that it changes**: What means something at twenty-five won't be the same at forty, and that's normal. Meaning needs periodic refocusing; it isn't something you find once and use for life.

### One thing to watch out for

A sense of meaning is not the same as happiness. Something can be deeply meaningful without making you happy or comfortable, and this point is crucial. You also have to think about which you value more — living happily or living meaningfully. Achieving both is of course ideal. To put it in the plainest terms: playing video games won't put food on the table.

## How the Five Dimensions Interlock

I started out just listing five points, but as I organized them I realized they actually have a structure:

| Dimension         | The question it answers                    | What happens without it                                     |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sense of meaning  | Why am I doing any of this                 | Everything feels hollow                                     |
| Continuous growth | Do I still have possibilities              | Stagnation, feeling stuck                                   |
| Sense of giving   | Am I of value to others                    | Low sense of self-worth                                     |
| Deep connection   | Is anyone there to catch the real me       | Long-term loneliness, always having to perform              |
| Self-awareness    | What's actually going on with me right now | You can't adjust the other four, because you can't see them |

**Meaning** is the direction, **growth** and **giving** are the actions, **deep connection** is the support, and **self-awareness** is the feedback loop — it's the one that tells you whether the state of the other four is healthy right now.

So if you ask where to start, my answer would be self-awareness. Because until you can see your own state, you don't even know which of them you're missing.

## Summary

Spiritual fulfillment isn't a state so much as a **balance**. There's never a day when it's "achieved" — there's only the difference between "reasonably balanced lately" and "a bit off lately."

And the most counterintuitive part is this: not one of the five requires much money. What they require is attention — and attention happens to be the scarcest thing modern people have, and the easiest thing to have stolen from them.

So the next time you come across the phrase "spiritual fulfillment," you don't have to write it off as fluff. Break it into five boxes, see which of your boxes is empty, and go fill that one.
